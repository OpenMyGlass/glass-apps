# Voice: hotword, commands, dictation (decision record)

Researched 2026-10-21 for an Apache-2.0 project that others may redistribute, running offline on a Cortex-A9 at 1 GHz (NEON) with 1 or 2 GB of RAM. The licence of the **code** and of the **model weights and training data** are checked separately. CPU figures are estimates scaled from Raspberry Pi and ESP32 reports, not measurements.

## Decision

1. **Hotword:** microWakeWord's streaming model format and runtime. We train our own phrase:
   - positives from [piper-sample-generator](https://github.com/rhasspy/piper-sample-generator) (MIT) with voices whose model cards are permissive (checked one by one, some Piper voices are NC);
   - negatives regenerated from permissive sources only: LibriSpeech, VOiCES, MUSAN (CC BY 4.0), Common Voice (CC0), our own recordings. Not the published NC features, not WHAM, not the NC parts of FSD50K and FMA.
   - The trained weights, the data list and the training recipe are published with the model.
2. **Commands after the hotword:** Vosk small-en-us with a fixed grammar (`take a picture`, `record a video`, `send a message to`, `take a note`, `[unk]`). It runs only after the hotword fires.
3. **Dictation:** on the phone, through the OpenMyGlass companion (glass-services dictation request → companion link → bridge `DictationStart` / `AudioChunk` / `Transcript`). Large-vocabulary recognition on the A9 is too slow and too memory-hungry.
4. The engines sit behind a `HotwordDetector` and a `CommandRecognizer` trait, so Rustpotter or sherpa-onnx can replace them.

## Decisions for the owner

- **The phrase.** "ok glass" is Google's phrase and possibly a trademark; the model NOTICE grants no trademark rights. Proposed: our own phrase (for example "okay Glass" variants or "hey Glass"), after a trademark check, or keep "ok glass" for personal builds only.
- **Training the model** needs a machine with a GPU or several CPU hours, and about 50–100 GB of temporary disk for the datasets. Not done yet.

## Platform notes

- Every runtime above is built from source for musl (route 2); the prebuilt armv7 Linux binaries are glibc.
- Android: NDK r26 and later need API 21 or newer, which fits the 5.1 and 6.0 targets.
